Privacy Policy

Offline AI Reminder for iPhone and iPad · last updated 23 September 2026

Offline AI Reminder collects nothing, sends nothing and has no accounts. Your reminders, the times you finished them and your score exist only on your device. There is no server to send them to, because the app has no networking code in it at all.

This is checkable, not just claimed

Every privacy policy says something like the above. This one can be tested in about ten seconds, and you should test it:

  • Put the device in Airplane Mode and launch the app. Everything works identically — including the assistant, which is the part that would need a server in almost any other app.
  • Open Settings → Offline AI Reminder. There is no "Wireless Data" or "Cellular Data" row, because iOS only shows one for apps that have actually used the network.
  • The app never shows a "would like to find and connect to devices on your local network" prompt, because it never looks.

The language model that answers you is a file inside the app bundle. It is the reason the download is large, and it is the reason nothing has to leave.

What the app stores, and where

WhatWhereLeaves the device?
Your reminders — titles, notes, dates, repeat rules, lists A file in the app's own containerNo
What you have ticked off, and when The same file, filed by the day it was dueNo
Your points, streak and level Computed from the above; nothing extra is storedNo
Your settings — theme, week start, notification preferences The app's own preferencesNo
What you type to the assistant In memory, for the length of the conversationNo

A second copy of your reminder file is kept beside the first, so that a damaged file does not cost you your history. Both are inside the app's own container and both go when the app does.

If a file ever turns out to be unreadable, it is moved aside and kept rather than deleted, under a dated name in that same container — on the principle that data you cannot read is still yours, and throwing it away is not the app's decision to make. It goes when the app is deleted, like everything else.

Nothing is written to a log that leaves the device. The app has no crash reporter and no diagnostics upload; on the rare failure path it prints a line to the device's own console, which never leaves the phone and which nobody but you can read.

All of it is deleted when you delete the app. There is nothing left behind and nothing held anywhere else.

The assistant

The assistant runs a language model on your device. What you type goes to that model and nowhere else.

It is also worth knowing how little of the work the model actually does. Dates, times, repeat rules, counts and points are worked out by the app itself — deterministically, from your own data. The model's only job is to add a sentence of its own underneath, and a sentence containing any figure is discarded before you see it. That is a correctness decision rather than a privacy one, but it has the same effect: your reminders are never handed to a language model to be re-described.

You can switch the model's contributions off entirely in Settings → Assistant → Written notes. The assistant keeps working, because the understanding was never the model's job.

What the app does not do

  • No account, no sign-up, no email address, no phone number
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no telemetry of any kind
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, no App Tracking Transparency prompt — there is nothing to track
  • No third-party SDKs
  • No access to your contacts, photos, location, microphone, camera, calendar or health data
  • No iCloud, no sync, no backup to anywhere the app controls
  • No data is sold, shared, rented or disclosed, because none is collected

Permissions

The app asks for exactly one thing.

PermissionAsked whenUsed for
Notifications The first time you have a reminder, not at launch Telling you a reminder is due. Nothing else.

If you say no, the app keeps working — your reminders are all still there and the calendar and score are unaffected. Nothing will pop up to tell you about them, which is most of the point of a reminder app, so it is worth saying yes. You can change your mind in iOS Settings at any time.

Reminders you mark high priority are delivered at the Time Sensitive level, which is what lets them through a Focus. Normal and low ones are not, deliberately: an app that marks everything urgent has marked nothing urgent.

Children and age rating

The app is not directed at children and collects nothing from anybody. It does include an on-device assistant that answers free text, so it is not rated 4+, and that is the honest answer rather than a convenient one.

Your rights

Data-protection law gives you rights of access, correction, deletion, portability and objection. They are satisfied here by construction rather than by process: the only copy of your data is on your device, in your hands. Deleting the app deletes it. There is nothing to request from anybody, because nobody else has anything.
